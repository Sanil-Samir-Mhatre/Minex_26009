# Recent **AI/ML** Methods and **Multispectral** Limits

Recent manganese mineral prospectivity mapping uses three linked method families: **multi-source ML classifiers**, newer **data-scarcity deep/semi-supervised models**, and **remote-sensing feature extraction** from multispectral plus, increasingly, hyperspectral data. The main limitation of satellite multispectral data is not usefulness but specificity: it can map host rocks, structures, iron oxides, and some alteration well, yet it often cannot uniquely identify manganese minerals or concealed ore without geological, geochemical, geophysical, and field constraints.  (Zhao et al., 2025; Singh et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2025; Sikakwe, 2023; Khurram et al., 2025)## AI/ML Methods

The dominant 2020–2026 pattern is **supervised learning on fused evidence layers** built from geology, structures, geochemistry, geophysics, and remotely sensed products rather than raw imagery alone  (Sun et al., 2020; Senanayake et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023; Mitra et al., 2025). In manganese-specific work, the strongest recent example integrated multi-source remote sensing with machine learning in the Malkansu belt and found high performance for RF, Naïve Bayes, and XGBoost, with AUCs of 0.975, 0.983, and 0.916, then combined them into an 11-zone favorability map  (Zhao et al., 2025). That study also found **structural density and lithology** were the most influential predictors, with linear structures aligned to the manganese belt  (Zhao et al., 2025).

- **Random Forest** is one of the most repeatedly strong and robust MPM models, often excelling in predictive efficiency even when CNN or SVM can match or exceed training accuracy  (Sun et al., 2020; Lachaud et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023).
- **Ensembling** is increasingly common, either by overlaying RF/SVM/other model outputs or combining multiple learners to raise confidence and stabilize predictions  (Senanayake et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023; Mitra et al., 2025).
- **Hyperparameter optimization** materially improves model performance in prospectivity mapping, with optimized models commonly pushing AUC above 0.8 and sometimes much higher  (Lin et al., 2021).

## Emerging Models for Sparse Data

A major recent shift is toward models designed for **limited labeled deposits**, because mineral prospectivity datasets are small and imbalanced relative to mainstream ML tasks  (Sun et al., 2020; Amirajlo et al., 2026). Deep forest was proposed as a non-neural deep model for remote-sensing-based prospectivity and achieved AUC 0.964 with fewer than 15 occurrences in one study, specifically targeting the early-exploration small-sample problem  (Taha et al., 2024). For manganese itself, a 2025 Songtao study used **Geo-WGAN plus biased-SVM plus Geo-DCNN** to generate synthetic ore-bearing samples, correct class imbalance, and improve generalization for deeper concealed Mn deposits  (Kong et al., 2025).

- **Semi-supervised and generative methods** are now used to handle scarce positive manganese samples and hidden deep targets  (Kong et al., 2025).
- **Calibration and reproducibility** matter as much as peak AUC under extreme scarcity; grid search sometimes yields more stable probabilities than Bayesian optimization  (Amirajlo et al., 2026).
- **Interpretability-aware deep models** are also emerging, including attention CNNs, self-attention architectures, graph neural networks, and autoencoder-plus-ensemble hybrids  (Amirajlo et al., 2026).

The workflow that many studies now converge on is visible in comparative and review papers: generate evidential layers from remote sensing and other geodata, train several ML models, validate with ROC/success-rate curves, then combine outputs into a consensus prospectivity map. Recent manganese exploration still uses **multispectral enhancement** heavily, especially ASTER and Sentinel-2 products such as band ratios, PCA, SAM, and constrained-energy or matched-filter style anomaly extraction  (Singh et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2025; Khurram et al., 2025). In central India, VIS-NIR-SWIR multisensor optical data plus L-band microwave data were integrated, and the resulting anomaly maps correlated well with existing manganese mines  (Singh et al., 2023). In Pakistan, ASTER differentiated lithological units effectively, but **Hyperion hyperspectral data** were the more decisive tool for recognizing Mn minerals such as psilomelane and braunite through spectral matching  (Khurram et al., 2025).

- **ASTER often outperforms other multispectral sensors** for alteration mapping, while Sentinel-2 and Landsat are widely used for regional screening and lithological contrast  (Sikakwe, 2023; Daoud et al., 2025; Shirmard et al., 2022).
- **Hyperspectral data** provide narrower bands and better mineral discrimination than multispectral systems, especially for direct mineral identification  (Hajaj et al., 2024; Sikakwe, 2023; Daoud et al., 2025).
- **Sensor fusion** generally gives more credible exploration results than any single sensor alone  (Sikakwe, 2023; Shirmard et al., 2021; Tang et al., 2025).

## Multispectral Limitations

The main remaining limitation of satellite multispectral data for manganese exploration is that it usually captures **indirect proxies** better than manganese ore itself: lithology, structures, iron staining, hydroxyl-bearing alteration, vegetation or terrain contrasts, and weathered surface expressions  (Tang et al., 2025; Daoud et al., 2025). That is why manganese studies repeatedly validate remote-sensing outputs with lab spectra, petrography, field mapping, geochemistry, or existing mine locations rather than treating image anomalies as ore proof  (Singh et al., 2023; Khurram et al., 2025).

- **Spectral specificity is limited** in multispectral data; hyperspectral data are better for direct Mn mineral recognition because of narrower bands and richer absorption detail  (Sikakwe, 2023; Khurram et al., 2025).
- **Surface and weathering effects** can mislead interpretation, because Mn anomalies can reflect secondary geochemical processes and redox sensitivity rather than primary ore controls  (Sun et al., 2020).
- **Concealed and deep deposits** remain difficult for optical satellites, which is why newer manganese studies target weak information recovery, sample augmentation, and integration with subsurface geoscience layers  (Kong et al., 2025).

Recent work also points to broader technical limits: uncertainty quantification is still underdeveloped in remote-sensing ML, advanced algorithms remain constrained by data scarcity, and high-resolution hyperspectral data are more informative but costlier and slower to process than standard multispectral imagery  (Shirmard et al., 2022; Tang et al., 2025; Wan et al., 2021).

Recent 2020–2026 manganese prospectivity mapping therefore favors **multi-source ML ensembles** and, where data are sparse or deposits are concealed, newer **semi-supervised, generative, and deep-forest/deep-learning approaches**. Satellite multispectral data remain valuable for regional targeting, especially for structures, lithology, and alteration, but they are usually insufficient alone for reliable manganese exploration without higher-spectral-resolution data and ground-validated multi-source integration.

# **Machine Learning** for Mineral Prospectivity

Recent research extensively explores multi-source geospatial data fusion, emerging architectures for sparse deposits, and critical limitations surrounding underexplored data combinations  (Abedini et al., 2023; Yuan et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2025).

Recent mineral prospectivity mapping (MPM) integrates remote sensing, geology, geochemistry, and geophysics to locate mineralized bodies  (Senanayake et al., 2023; Mitra et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2023). However, substantial gaps remain in establishing physical coupling across disparate data layers  (Yuan et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2025).

## Multi-Source Data Integration

Recent MPM frameworks process high-dimensional datasets into standardized predictor layers capturing source, pathway, and trap processes  (Shirmard et al., 2021; Sun et al., 2019; Heidari et al., 2026). Optical satellite sensors like ASTER, Sentinel-2, and hyperspectral platforms extract alteration indices such as phyllic, argillic, or propylitic alteration  (Tang et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2023). These remote sensing layers are stacked alongside bedrock lithologies, structural fault buffers, and multielement stream or soil geochemical anomalies  (Tang et al., 2025; He et al., 2024).

- Ensembles of tree-based models and shallow classifiers consistently identify prospective belts with high spatial efficiency  (Tang et al., 2025; Senanayake et al., 2023; Mitra et al., 2025).
- Random forest algorithms frequently yield superior stability and interpretability over non-ensemble counterparts  (Mahboob et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2020).
- Subsurface geophysics, such as gravity and magnetics, complement surficial geochemical and spectral alteration footprints  (Heidari et al., 2026; Senanayake et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2023).

## Advanced Learning Architectures

Data sparsity and extreme class imbalance remain standard constraints in prospectivity modeling because economic mineral occurrences are rare  (Tang et al., 2025; Li et al., 2024; Mantilla-Dulcey et al., 2024). To counter label scarcity, recent workflows leverage positive-unlabeled bagging, semi-supervised formulations, and generative adversarial frameworks  (Heidari et al., 2026; Li et al., 2024; Mantilla-Dulcey et al., 2024). Deep learning architectures like convolutional networks and deep autoencoders extract latent multi-scale spatial representations across complex non-linear evidential inputs  (Ghane et al., 2026; Xiong et al., 2018; Fu et al., 2023). 

- Deep convolutional neural networks achieve test accuracies exceeding 95% on multi-element geochemical and remote sensing grids  (Fu et al., 2023; He et al., 2024).
- Semi-supervised generative adversarial models capture more deposits within small prospective tracts than standard supervised baselines  (Li et al., 2024).
- Unsupervised vision Transformers and autoencoders extract latent features without requiring manual label engineering  (Ning et al., 2025; Ghane et al., 2026).

Workflows frequently convert multi-source geoinformation into distinct evidential raster layers that feed into machine learning models for anomaly prediction. A representative structure of this multi-source data processing pipeline illustrates how diverse inputs are unified. **Figure 1:** Pipeline diagram showing machine learning integration of remote sensing and geodata  (Shirmard et al., 2021)## Underexplored Data Combinations

Despite progress in empirical stack integration, several key data pairings remain poorly addressed in the literature  (Yuan et al., 2026; Tang et al., 2025). Remote sensing alteration extractions are rarely reconciled against vertical geochemical zonality or thermodynamic dispersion haloes  (Abedini et al., 2023). Furthermore, quantitative geomorphological indices and digital elevation derivatives are seldom coupled dynamically with structural kinematics or deep geophysical surveys  (Tang et al., 2025).

- Genetic links between quantitative terrain indices and subsurface hydrothermal alteration systems remain largely unformalized  (Tang et al., 2025).
- Spaceborne hyperspectral data remain underutilized alongside multi-element lithogeochemistry due to processing expense and calibration gaps  (Tang et al., 2025; Hajaj et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2023).
- Systematic integration of vertical zonality ratios with satellite-derived surface mineral indices remains poorly explored  (Abedini et al., 2023).

# **Hyperspectral** gives **more specific mineral ID**; multispectral is effective for alteration screening and manganese targeting, but indirect.

Recent studies split into **sensor-specific mineral mapping**, **manganese exploration workflows**, and **what multispectral data still miss**  (Shirkhani et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025; El-Omairi & Garouani, 2026).

## Sensor Use

Sentinel-2 and Landsat-8 are widely used to map **iron oxides, hydroxyl-bearing minerals, silicates, and some carbonates** from VNIR-SWIR band ratios, PCA, and SAM, especially for alteration mapping rather than direct ore identification  (Adiri et al., 2020; Habtemichael et al., 2026; Singh et al., 2023).

ASTER remains especially strong for **OH-bearing and carbonate minerals** because its six SWIR bands resolve those absorption features better than Sentinel-2 or Landsat-8 in many settings  (Sekandari et al., 2020; Chniouar et al., 2024; Mohsin et al., 2025).

Sentinel-2 often performs best for **iron-bearing minerals** because its VNIR placement and 10 m pixels improve mapping of hematite and related ferric features  (Kouzeli et al., 2026; Chniouar et al., 2024).

Very high spatial resolution does not guarantee better mineral discrimination if spectral coverage is narrow: WV-3 VNIR was sensitive mainly to **ferric iron and Mn oxides/hydroxides**, while Sentinel-2 captured a broader Fe-Mn alteration suite because it also includes SWIR bands  (Sykioti et al., 2023).

Hyperspectral sensors such as Hyperion, PRISMA, ZY-1E, and EnMap are used when studies need **directer mineral identification**, endmember extraction, and spectral matching against libraries or field spectra  (Khurram et al., 2025; Shirkhani et al., 2025; Mohsin et al., 2025; Van Der Meer et al., 2012).

A comparison figure from one recent study shows how Sentinel-2 and WorldView-3 differed in mapped Fe-Mn-related absorptions, illustrating that spectral coverage can outweigh finer pixel size. **Figure 1:** Sentinel-2 absorption maps for Fe-Mn alteration minerals  (Sykioti et al., 2023)- **Band ratios and PCA** remain standard for multispectral enhancement  (Mayappan, 2019; Sikakwe, 2023).
- **SAM, SFF, MNF, and matched filtering** are common for hyperspectral classification  (Wan et al., 2021; Shirkhani et al., 2025).
- **Field spectra, XRD, petrography, and geochemistry** are routinely used for validation  (Shirkhani et al., 2025; Singh et al., 2023; Kouzeli et al., 2026).

## Manganese Exploration

Recent manganese studies use multispectral data mainly to map **host lithologies, structures, and alteration proxies**, then combine them with field or lab constraints to target ore  (Yang et al., 2024; Singh et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2025).

In central India, ASTER and Sentinel-2 were combined with ALOS PALSAR, Landsat-derived PCs, gravity gradients, lab spectra, and known mines to produce a manganese prospect map, showing the value of multi-sensor fusion over imagery alone  (Singh et al., 2023).

In the Malkansu manganese belt, Sentinel-2, Landsat-8, GF-2, ASTER, and DEM data were fused with geology and machine learning; PCA and SAM extracted anomalies including **hydroxyl, chlorite, rhodochrosite, soft manganese, and pyrolusite-related signals** that aligned with faults and known deposits  (Zhao et al., 2025).

For concealed “Datangpo-type” manganese in Guizhou, one study targeted **associated pyrite, chlorite, and secondary manganese oxide** as remote-sensing markers, then used PCA, SVM, and ant-colony optimization to delineate anomalous zones consistent with known deposits  (Xu et al., 2020).

Hyperspectral manganese studies go further by identifying specific Mn minerals. In Pakistan, Hyperion matched sample spectra to **psilomelane** with perfect ACE and likelihood scores and supported mapping of braunite-bearing mineralization, while ASTER handled lithologic discrimination more than direct Mn mineral ID  (Khurram et al., 2025).

- **Landsat** is often used for lineament extraction in Mn belts  (Yang et al., 2024).
- **ASTER and Sentinel-2** are used for alteration anomalies around Mn mineralization  (Singh et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2025).
- **Hyperspectral data** enable mineral-specific Mn matches such as psilomelane  (Khurram et al., 2025).

## Remaining Limits

The main mineralogical information still difficult to obtain from multispectral imagery is **unambiguous, mineral-specific identification**, especially where minerals share broad absorption features or occur in mixed pixels  (Chniouar et al., 2024; El-Omairi & Garouani, 2026).

Multispectral sensors can often separate classes like iron oxides versus hydroxyl-bearing alteration, but they struggle to distinguish **closely related Mn, clay, carbonate, and mixed alteration assemblages** with the specificity achievable from hyperspectral spectra  (Habtemichael et al., 2026; Chniouar et al., 2024; Khurram et al., 2025).

They also remain weak for **blind, buried, or deep mineralization**, because optical EO records surface expressions rather than subsurface ore directly  (Sikakwe, 2023; Van Der Meer et al., 2012; Xu et al., 2020).

Validation remains a bottleneck: spectral libraries vary, natural heterogeneity and spectral mixing distort signatures, and preprocessing is not standardized across studies  (Peyghambari & Zhang, 2021; Wan et al., 2021; El-Omairi & Garouani, 2026).

- **Spectral confusion** limits mineral-group separation in multispectral data  (Chniouar et al., 2024; El-Omairi & Garouani, 2026).
- **Mixed pixels and spatial resolution** can offset spectral advantages or disadvantages  (Kouzeli et al., 2026; Wan et al., 2021).
- **Ground truth remains essential** for reliable mineral maps  (Sekandari et al., 2020; Wan et al., 2021; Shirkhani et al., 2025).

Recent work therefore uses Sentinel-2, Landsat, ASTER, SAR, DEMs, and hyperspectral imagery as a **complementary stack**: multispectral data screen structures and alteration efficiently, while hyperspectral data provide the **mineralogical specificity** that multispectral imagery still usually cannot  (Sikakwe, 2023; Hajaj et al., 2024; El-Omairi & Garouani, 2026).

# **Geophysical Derivatives** and Structural Features

Beyond standard vegetation indices, raw elevation, and proximity to known deposits, recent AI-driven prospectivity studies identify prospective zones using **geophysical transform derivatives**, **structural and fractal complexity metrics**, and **knowledge embeddings** with multi-element geochemistry.

Recent machine learning models integrate multi-source evidential layers capturing ore-forming plumbing, structural traps, and geochemical dispersion  (Senanayake et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2025; Ghane et al., 2026). These sophisticated inputs enable models to delineate concealed or blind mineral occurrences across complex terranes  (Senanayake et al., 2023; Ordóñez et al., 2024; Ali et al., 2026).

## Geophysical Transform Derivatives

Processed potential-field and radiometric data reveal subsurface fluid conduits and concealed intrusive architectures beneath cover.

- **Vertical magnetic derivatives** such as the first vertical derivative pinpoint magnetic contrasts driven by fluid flow and mineralization  (Heidari et al., 2026; Ordóñez et al., 2024; Elfadly et al., 2026).
- **Radiometric element ratios**, particularly thorium-to-potassium, reliably characterize hydrothermal alteration signatures associated with ore-forming intrusions  (Heidari et al., 2026; Ordóñez et al., 2024; Elfadly et al., 2026).
- **Deep electromagnetic datasets**, including magnetotelluric and airborne EM resistivity grids, constrain deep crustal conduits  (Senanayake et al., 2023).

## Structural and Fractal Metrics

Structural density, directional lineaments, and nonlinear scaling metrics isolate deformation corridors that focus hydrothermal fluids.

# **Uncertainty Quantification** and Explainable AI

Recent research decomposes mineral prospectivity into uncertainty estimation methods, explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) frameworks, and decision-support improvements over static prospectivity rankings  (Zhang et al., 2024; Mou et al., 2023; Nagasingha et al., 2025).


**Figure 1:** Consensus on uncertainty-aware maps improving mineral exploration decision-making

The literature strongly agrees that uncertainty-aware prospectivity mapping mitigates exploration risk and improves spatial selectivity relative to deterministic rankings  (Yang et al., 2022; Wang & Zuo, 2026; Nagasingha et al., 2025).

## Uncertainty Quantification Methods

Uncertainty in data-driven prospectivity modeling arises from noisy or sparse geospatial datasets (aleatoric uncertainty), model and algorithmic design choices (epistemic uncertainty), and broader data science workflow configurations  (Zhang et al., 2024). Recent frameworks model these variations using ensemble bootstrapping, stochastic simulation of evidence layers, and Bayesian deep learning  (Mou et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2022; Wang & Zuo, 2026). Direct sampling algorithms simulate equiprobable evidence layers to evaluate input data sensitivity, while conditional variational autoencoders (CVAEs) derive aleatoric spreads from posterior distributions and epistemic variations from multi-realization predictive runs  (Yang et al., 2022; Nagasingha et al., 2025).

- **Bayesian deep learning** separates predictive uncertainty into aleatoric and epistemic components using Metropolis–Hastings sampling or variational inference over neural network weights  (Liu, 2025).
- **Ensemble resampling** and Monte Carlo dropout quantify parameter variability and model sensitivity across repeated predictive runs  (Ghane et al., 2026; Wang & Zuo, 2026).
- **Direct sampling simulation** models data uncertainty originating from low spatial resolution and subjective interpretations of evidence maps  (Yang et al., 2022; Wang & Zuo, 2026).

## Explainable AI Frameworks

Black-box predictions often lack credibility among exploration geoscientists because spatial associations cannot be easily reconciled with metallogenic processes  (Mou et al., 2023; Zuo et al., 2024; Yin et al., 2024). Modern prospectivity studies implement model-agnostic attribution algorithms and domain knowledge embeddings to explain predictions  (Mou et al., 2023; Zuo et al., 2024). Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) and Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) quantify global and local feature contributions, determining whether deep neural networks or ensemble classifiers rely on genuine ore controls such as fault dilation tendency, intrusive boundaries, and hydrothermal alteration  (Yu et al., 2024; Saduov et al., 2026; Yin et al., 2024). 

Multi-model agreement can also be evaluated spatially by combining diverse algorithms into consensus distributions, illustrating regional stability and entropy across model structures  (Daviran et al., 2022; Yousefi et al., 2024). **Figure 2:** Spatial entropy model illustrating predictive disagreement across multiple prospectivity algorithms  (Yousefi et al., 2024)- **Shapley Additive Explanations** rank the relative influence of multi-source predictors and verify geological associations across predictive surfaces  (Ghane et al., 2026; Saduov et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2024).
- **Partial dependence plots** and individual conditional expectation plots trace marginal feature responses to establish physical consistency with deposit models  (Mou et al., 2023; Yin et al., 2024).
- **Domain knowledge embeddings** guide model training from input data structuring to loss formulations, enhancing interpretability and generalizability  (Zuo et al., 2024; Yin et al., 2024).

## Decision Support Beyond Ternary Maps

Deterministic high/medium/low prospectivity maps typically delineate broad prospective areas that are biased toward well-known deposits while concealing severe underlying prediction errors  (Mou et al., 2023; Wang & Zuo, 2026; Lauzon & Gloaguen, 2024). Decoupling prospectivity value from confidence enables exploration teams to distinguish robust, drill-ready targets from permissive tracts requiring further baseline sampling  (Liu, 2025; Wang & Zuo, 2026; Yousefi et al., 2024).

| Analytical Dimension | Deterministic Prospectivity | Uncertainty-Aware Decision Mapping | Citations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Search Space Reduction** | Yields expansive, overconfident target zones | Narrows drilling tracts to ~3–5% of the total area |  (Mou et al., 2023; Daviran et al., 2022)|
| **Model Calibration** | Uses uncalibrated classification probabilities | Employs isotonic regression to minimize Brier scores |  (Saduov et al., 2026)|
| **Domain Validity** | Extrapolates into untested feature domains | Masks predictions outside the Area of Applicability |  (Saduov et al., 2026)|
| **Portfolio Allocation** | Merges diverse risk regimes into single scores | Applies Markowitz and Getis–Ord risk-return categorizations |  (Yang et al., 2022; Nagasingha et al., 2025)|

**Figure 3:** Comparison of deterministic classifications against uncertainty-aware decision mapping frameworks

Ensemble-modulated modeling coupled with accuracy–rejection curves establishes quantitative uncertainty thresholds for target selection  (Zhang et al., 2024; Mou et al., 2023). By integrating probability surfaces with confidence indices and majority voting, exploration managers can prioritize low-risk tracts that capture over 80% to 94% of known deposits while identifying high-uncertainty zones that warrant secondary geophysical surveys rather than immediate drilling  (Wang & Zuo, 2026; Nagasingha et al., 2025; Yousefi et al., 2024).

Uncertainty-aware and explainable AI transforms mineral prospectivity mapping from opaque classification into transparent risk-return optimization  (Wang & Zuo, 2026; Zuo et al., 2024; Nagasingha et al., 2025).

# **Machine Learning** Predicts Mining Shortfalls

Recent research on predicting mining production shortfalls addresses three core dimensions: **machine learning predictive architectures**, **operational constraint integration**, and **systemic model limitations**.

Modern operations predict production disruptions by analyzing multi-source telematics, meteorological events, and mechanical failure histories  (Guérin et al., 2025; Martins & Soofastaei, 2020). These predictive frameworks increasingly replace traditional static driver trees to optimize short-term planning under stochastic industrial conditions  (Guérin et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024).

## Machine Learning Predictive Architectures

Recent predictive workflows deploy ensembles and deep neural networks to forecast fleet capacity, crusher throughput, and delay events using real-time dispatch telematics  (Guérin et al., 2025; Kumah et al., 2024; Baek & Choi, 2020). Supervised regressors map nonlinear degradation signals and mechanical cycles directly to hourly tonnage outputs  (George & Nojabaei, 2023; Kumah et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025; Rahmah et al., 2024).

- **Deep neural networks** forecast open-pit haulage production with mean absolute percentage errors between 4.23% and 11.40% across operational shifts  (George & Nojabaei, 2023; Kumah et al., 2024; Baek & Choi, 2020).
- **Tree-based gradient boosting** algorithms, including XGBoost and CatBoost, achieve high accuracy (up to 85–95% $R^2$) predicting hauling capacity and stope output  (George & Nojabaei, 2023; Yang et al., 2025; Nobahar & Pourrahimian, 2022; Rahmah et al., 2024).
- **Two-stage classification-forecasting** pipelines resolve zero-production days in intermittent extraction by combining Random Forest classifiers with recurrent networks like Bi-LSTM  (Rahmah et al., 2024).

## Operational Constraint Integration

Forecasting models incorporate external weather, maintenance scheduling, and blast cycles into dynamic dispatch and capacity models  (Guérin et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Nobahar & Pourrahimian, 2022). Machine learning models trace dependencies across environmental factors and equipment wear to mitigate throughput deficits  (Guérin et al., 2025; George & Nojabaei, 2023; Elmouttie et al., 2021; Mulyono, 2026). The relationship between multi-variable telemetry and loading performance can be quantified to identify optimal cycle dynamics. **Figure 1:** Correlation coefficient heat map across loader performance and operational variables  (George & Nojabaei, 2023)- **Precipitation and weather** variables directly reduce haulage capacity, with heavy rainfall historically driving up to 10% in annual production losses  (Guérin et al., 2025; George & Nojabaei, 2023; Rahmah et al., 2024).
- **Condition-based predictive maintenance** using fluid chemistry and component telemetry prevents catastrophic downtime, achieving up to 98% diagnostic accuracy  (Alvars et al., 2026; Madahana et al., 2024).
- **Dynamic scheduling models** reschedule unexecuted production tasks following sudden breakdowns, cutting shovel relocation production loss by 54%  (Tu et al., 2023; Both & Dimitrakopoulos, 2020).

## Systemic Model Limitations

Despite strong benchmark accuracy, machine learning approaches face substantial practical limitations in capturing end-to-end mining environments  (Yang et al., 2025; Both & Dimitrakopoulos, 2020; Alvars et al., 2026; Mulyono, 2026). Disjointed operational data silos, class imbalance in component failure logs, and unmodeled human factors persistently degrade predictive reliability on site  (Guérin et al., 2025; Kannan et al., 2022; Elmouttie et al., 2021; Alvars et al., 2026).

- **Non-repair downtime delays**, such as workshop transit and diagnostic waiting, constitute up to 50% of total outage duration but are routinely omitted from models  (Dashtaki et al., 2025).
- **Geotechnical and blasting** variables remain severely underrepresented in fleet models, despite their verified influence on dozer efficiency and wall scaling  (Guérin et al., 2025; Elmouttie et al., 2021).
- **Data fragmentation** across legacy databases and absence of streaming IoT sensors restrict real-time remaining useful life estimation  (Kannan et al., 2022; Elmouttie et al., 2021; Mulyono, 2026; Rahimdel & Mohammadpour, 2025).

Integrating hybrid deep learning with real-time maintenance telemetry substantially improves shortfall foresight, yet widespread industrial adoption requires unifying geotechnical, blasting, and human constraints into automated decision systems  (Guérin et al., 2025; Elmouttie et al., 2021; Mulyono, 2026).

# **No unified AI system** integrates exploration and operations


**Figure 1:** Scientific consensus on the complete integration of exploration and operational mining AI systems

Current research confirms that while isolated sub-systems link geological grade uncertainty to scheduling or haulage telematics to weather, no single decision-support system bridges macro-scale mineral prospectivity mapping with granular short-term mine operations  (Rahimi, 2025; Chimunhu et al., 2024). This disconnect decomposes into three distinct facets: existing partial integrations, operational domain silos, and persistent architectural gaps.

## Partial AI Integrations

Recent decision-support systems link downstream geological uncertainty with dynamic extraction sequences and haulage simulation rather than regional prospectivity maps  (Rahimi, 2025; Both & Dimitrakopoulos, 2020; Kumar et al., 2020). Variational autoencoders generate probabilistic grade realizations that feed metaheuristic optimizers to evaluate schedules under spatial uncertainty  (Rahimi, 2025). Haulage frameworks combine telemetry with meteorological records and mechanical breakdown simulations to forecast production capacity  (Guérin et al., 2025).

- Continuous updating engines combine extended Kalman filtering and reinforcement learning to adapt short-term material routing dynamically  (Kumar et al., 2020).
- Stochastic integer formulations optimize extraction sequences and truck fleets jointly, reducing shovel relocation losses by 54%  (Both & Dimitrakopoulos, 2020).
- Geometallurgical frameworks integrate machine learning with geostatistics to adapt processing plant circuits under complex deposit variability  (Medina-Tasilla et al., 2024).

Predictive modeling for exploration prospectivity operates entirely separated from day-to-day operations and asset maintenance  (Katiyar et al., 2024; Chimunhu et al., 2024). Prospectivity systems focus strictly on macro-scale targeting using remote sensing, geophysics, and geochemical footprints without linking to operational logistics  (Yousefi et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2025; Senanayake et al., 2023; Echogdali et al., 2021). Concurrently, operational tools focus narrowly on fleet dispatching, equipment degradation, or worker ergonomics without incorporating upstream geological models  (Chimunhu et al., 2024; Choi et al., 2020; Misita et al., 2024; Rojas et al., 2025).

- Upstream prospectivity models stop at target delineation and drill-hole targeting rather than operational risk estimation  (Katiyar et al., 2024; Karbalaeiramezanali et al., 2025).
- Haulage forecasting tools neglect upstream blasting parameters, pit-wall stability reports, and geotechnical constraints  (Guérin et al., 2025; Chimunhu et al., 2024).
- Equipment predictive maintenance systems optimize component changeouts in isolation from short-term production targets  (Chimunhu et al., 2024; Rojas et al., 2025).

## Dynamic Scheduling and Fleet Allocation

Mining systems combine discrete-event simulation (DES) with deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to adapt production schedules when unexpected breakdowns, queue bottlenecks, or cycle shifts arise  (Seipolt et al., 2024; Yan et al., 2022; Noriega & Pourrahimian, 2024; Gan et al., 2023).

- **Deep Q-Learning shovel allocation** trained on discrete-event simulators learns robust three-month scheduling strategies that consistently hit crusher hourly targets under haulage uncertainty  (Noriega & Pourrahimian, 2024).
- **Hierarchical and multi-agent frameworks** separate maintenance selection from equipment dispatching using multi-head deep Q-networks, outperforming mathematical solvers in execution runtime  (Yan et al., 2022; Ouahabi et al., 2024; Rojas et al., 2025).
- **Digital twin synchronization** updates virtual representations of assets to enable live rescheduling during sudden disruptions, reducing deviations between planned and actual output  (Yan et al., 2022; Kuhn & Antonino, 2023; Tong et al., 2025).

Modern drill-and-blast operations utilize digital twins and mathematical programming to balance rock fragmentation against machine vibration, drill-rig wear, and haul-truck path navigation  (Perincek et al., 2025).

- **Non-uniform pattern optimization** varies hole burden and spacing to reflect rock hardness changes, boosting fragmentation by 3% to 4% while lowering overall hole counts  (Perincek et al., 2025).

Recent research integrates satellite Earth observation variables with mining operational records across three core facets: **ground deformation and geo-hazard forecasting**, **hydrological flooding and soil moisture modeling**, and **extreme weather and operational convergence risks**  (Qin et al., 2021; Kopec et al., 2020; Cruz & Da Silva, 2026; Cieślik et al., 2024).

## Geo-Hazard and Geotechnical Forecasting

Mining studies combine satellite radar interferometry, high-resolution optical imagery, and digital terrain models with extraction records to predict surface instability and infrastructure hazards  (Qin et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2026; Cieślik et al., 2024). Machine learning models map nonlinear interactions between excavation geometry, topography, and surface displacements to forecast slope failures and ground subsidence  (Qin et al., 2021; Cieślik et al., 2024).

- **Underground extraction parameters**, including mined seam thickness and timing of mining, explain satellite radar subsidence velocities when trained on XGBoost models with SHAP attribution  (Cieślik et al., 2024).
- **Random Forest classifiers** trained on high-resolution Gaofen-1 imagery, slope, aspect, and excavation elevation differences achieve 98.5% overall accuracy in identifying mining-induced geo-disasters  (Qin et al., 2021).
- **Topographic lineament extraction** via digital elevation models, combined with historical excavation boundaries, isolates areas susceptible to landslides and ground fissures  (Qin et al., 2021; Cieślik et al., 2024).

The literature landscape displays a pronounced structural divergence: while automated machine learning methods are applied to general exploration targeting  (Mkhohlakali et al., 2026; Sun et al., 2024)and short-term equipment management  (Azhari et al., 2023; Jung & Choi, 2021), unified decision-support platforms bridging remote sensing to shift dispatch remain nonexistent  (Nunes et al., 2026; Leung et al., 2023). For manganese specifically, models rely almost exclusively on static, multispectral lithological classifications  (Zhao et al., 2025), leaving dynamic operational variables entirely disconnected from resource models  (Liang et al., 2023; Nunes et al., 2026).

## Multimodal Fusion and Uncertainty Estimation

Current manganese exploration studies rely on static remote sensing overlays, failing to fuse deep multimodal features with rigorous uncertainty quantification  (Zhao et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025; Bali et al., 2026). Most prospective models apply basic algebraic or empirical weightings across satellite bands, which introduces subjective bias and leaves predictions uncalibrated against ground truth  (Zhao et al., 2025; Farahbakhsh et al., 2025).

- Machine learning pipelines in manganese belts integrate multi-sensor datasets such as ASTER, Sentinel-2, Landsat-8, and GF-2, where Naïve Bayes achieves an AUC of 0.983  (Zhao et al., 2025), yet they omit unified deep embeddings  (Zhao et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2026). Alteration mapping relies heavily on manual endmember matching for minerals like pyrolusite and rhodochrosite. **Figure 2:** Spectral angle mapper alteration extractions for pyrolusite and associated mineralization anomalies  (Zhao et al., 2025)- Current mineral prospectivity systems lack standardized validation frameworks, spatial cross-validation, and systematic geophysical correction pipelines  (Bali et al., 2026).
- Deep learning architectures rarely provide mathematical modeling of uncertainty bounds or operational degradation characteristics, discouraging industrial adoption  (Azhari et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2025).

## Temporal Monitoring and Production-Risk Prediction

Real-time mining workflows suffer from fragmented architectures that evaluate environmental hazards, machine telemetry, and production forecasts in isolation  (Ramadhan, 2025; Nunes et al., 2026). Predictive systems fail to track dynamic spatial changes or capture cascading production impacts resulting from extreme weather and geotechnical instability  (Arinez et al., 2020; Ramadhan, 2025; Nunes et al., 2026).

- Environmental early-warning systems, such as EcoRisk-AI, demonstrate that fusing spatiotemporal satellite imagery with IoT sensors improves hazard lead time by 24–72 hours  (Ramadhan, 2025).
- Integrating subsurface-surface deformation sensing through InSAR and pore-pressure telemetry reduces false negatives in slope-failure detection by 18% to 32%  (Ramadhan, 2025).
- Existing production planning models remain structural silos that ignore real-time meteorological impacts, dust emission levels, and ground-water interactions on haul routes  (Azhari et al., 2023; Bali et al., 2026; Nunes et al., 2026).

## Automated Decision Support and Mine Planning

Dynamic mine planning systems lack autonomous optimization capabilities capable of integrating multi-agent reasoning with physics-based constraints  (Nunes et al., 2026; Leung et al., 2023). Commercial platforms operate via rigid schedules that cannot adjust dynamically to unexpected equipment failures, ore grade fluctuations, or logistics disruptions  (Liang et al., 2023; Nunes et al., 2026; Leung et al., 2023).

- Intelligent systems predominantly process retrospective, offline telemetry rather than driving real-time online closed-loop optimization across haul fleets  (Azhari et al., 2023; Jung & Choi, 2021).
- Emerging Large Language Model (LLM) agents lack safety validation protocols and certified alignment frameworks required for high-risk mine-site decision-making  (Nunes et al., 2026).
- Modern operations lack a uniform data schema to merge visual model data with operational streaming parameters into a unified decision platform  (Liang et al., 2023).

Bridging these gaps requires an end-to-end platform that unifies multimodal orebody embeddings, physics-constrained spatial uncertainty, and real-time agentic dispatching  (Zhao et al., 2024; Liang et al., 2023; Nunes et al., 2026; Leung et al., 2023).
